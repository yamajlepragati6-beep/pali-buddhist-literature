1. Introduction to Buddhist Literature

Buddhist literature is a collection of texts containing the teachings, philosophy, stories and principles of Buddhism. It preserves the ideas and teachings associated with Gautama Buddha.

2. Oral Tradition

In the early Buddhist tradition, the teachings of Buddha were preserved through memorisation and oral recitation by monks. These teachings were later recorded in written form.

3. Pali Canon

The Pali Canon, also known as the Tipitaka, is the main collection of scriptures in the Theravada Buddhist tradition. It contains teachings about discipline, philosophy and Buddhist practice.

4. Important Buddhist Literary Works

  • Jataka Tales – stories about the previous lives of Buddha.
  • Dhammapada – verses containing ethical and spiritual teachings.
  • Milindapanha – a dialogue between King Milinda and the monk Nagasena.
  • Buddhavamsa – a text describing the lives of past Buddhas.

5. Contribution to Indian Culture

Buddhist literature contributed to Indian philosophy, ethics, education, art and culture. It also helped preserve knowledge about ancient Indian society and traditions.

6. Importance in Modern Times

Buddhist literature continues to be studied for its teachings on wisdom, compassion, moral values, peace and human conduct.
